About

Photographer · Himachal Pradesh, India

I was born in Himachal Pradesh — a state so naturally beautiful that I sometimes think it planted the seed for everything I do now, long before I ever picked up a camera.

The mountains, the light, the quiet — I didn't know it then, but growing up surrounded by that kind of beauty shapes how you look at things later in life. I moved away with my family when I was seven, for studies, and even the place I now call home is tucked in a town surrounded by mountains on all four sides. I suppose I never really left the mountains behind — they've just followed me from one home to the next.

Photography came much later, and purely as a hobby — just a phone in hand and a habit of noticing things. In the beginning, I shot in colour: everyday, ordinary moments, captured without much thought behind them.

Then one day I came across a black-and-white photograph online that stopped me completely. I don't even remember who posted it or where, but something about it — the way it stripped everything down to just light, shadow, and form — changed how I saw the world around me.

"That's when I stopped treating it as one-off posts and started building it properly — as a black-and-white photographer."

I'd been posting colour photos on Snapchat and Instagram for a while, just casually, without much response. Then, inspired by that photo, I posted my first monochrome shot on Instagram — and it did something my colour posts never had. People noticed it, liked it, responded to it in a way that told me I was onto something. That's when I stopped treating it as one-off posts and started building @tania_bnw properly, as a black-and-white photographer. It's still early — just three months into shooting seriously in monochrome, a little over 160 followers — but I've crossed 15,000 views in that time, and that's enough to tell me people are actually stopping to look.

My process is simple, really. I don't plan shoots or chase locations. I just observe — I'll be walking somewhere, notice light falling a certain way on a tree, or a building holding an interesting shadow, or a stranger caught mid-moment — and I take my phone out and click. That's it. No fancy gear, no elaborate setup. Just paying attention.

I want to be honest about one thing: this is, and I want it to stay, a passion project. I'll keep growing it — more followers, more people seeing the work — but not at the cost of what makes it feel like mine. I'm not chasing anything here except getting better and staying curious.

Photography isn't the only way I create, either. I also paint and sketch, and I've made a few charcoal portraits along the way — different medium, same instinct: noticing something and trying to hold onto it.

Every photo on this site is shot entirely on a phone, edited by hand, one frame at a time.
